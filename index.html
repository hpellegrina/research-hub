What it does

  • One list of tasks per project: to do, completed and archived
  • Every project's reports, newest first, one tap to open
  • Mark reports read, and see how many are unread per project
  • Archive tasks and reports you're done with
  • Add, rename, renumber and remove projects from the app
  • Reports stay in your shared folders; nothing is moved or copied
  • Your tasks and marks stay private in your own research_hub folder

Set up on your computer

  1. Download the zip above and double-click it to unzip. You get a folder called research_hub.
  2. Move that folder to the top level of your Dropbox, so it is Dropbox/research_hub.
  3. Mac: open research_hub/tracker and double-click Research Hub. The first time, your Mac may say it can't check the app: open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.
    Windows: double-click Open Research Hub (Windows).bat in the same folder.
  4. The hub opens in your browser. Click + Add project, give it a name, and click Choose folder… to pick the Dropbox folder where that project's PDFs are saved.
Troubleshooting

“python3” not found. On a Mac, accept the offer to install the command line developer tools, then open the app again. On Windows, install Python from python.org and tick “Add Python to PATH”.

A Terminal window stays open on a Mac. That's harmless; you can close it. To make it close by itself: Terminal › Settings › Profiles › Shell › “When the shell exits”: Close if the shell exited cleanly.

The Mac keeps blocking the app. Open Terminal and run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Dropbox/research_hub, then try again. This only removes the “downloaded from the internet” flag from this folder.

Set up on your phone

  1. On your phone, open this page and tap Open the phone app.
  2. iPhone: in Safari, tap Share › Add to Home Screen. Android: in Chrome, tap ⋮ › Add to Home screen.
  3. Open Research Hub from the new home-screen icon (on iPhone this matters: the icon keeps its own login).
  4. Paste a Dropbox app key (see below), tap Open Dropbox, allow access, copy the code Dropbox shows, and paste it back in the app. You stay connected after that.
Getting a Dropbox app key

The key lets the app open your Dropbox after you sign in. It never gives anyone else access to your files.

Easiest: ask the person who sent you this page for their key. (They need to click Enable additional users once, on their Dropbox app's Settings page.)

Or make your own (5 minutes, on a computer):

  1. Go to dropbox.com/developers/apps › Create app.
  2. Choose Scoped access and Full Dropbox. Name it anything unique, e.g. research-hub-yourname.
  3. On the Permissions tab, tick files.metadata.read, files.content.read and files.content.write, then Submit.
  4. On the Settings tab, copy the App key. Leave “Allow public clients” on Allow; no redirect URI is needed.

How the folder works

Everything is plain files, so you can also read or edit them in the Dropbox app.

Dropbox/
└── research_hub/
    ├── tracker/                  the app for your computer
    ├── projects/
    │   └── 1. Example project/
    │       ├── TASKS.md            to do, completed, archived
    │       ├── REPORTS.txt         where the PDFs are (a folder in Dropbox)
    │       ├── reports/            …or put the PDFs here instead
    │       ├── READ_REPORTS.txt    reports you've marked as read
    │       └── ARCHIVED_REPORTS.txt
    ├── removed_projects/         projects you removed (nothing is deleted)
    └── README.txt
